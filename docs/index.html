The summary trace (min / mean / max / noise per bucket) and per-layer statistics are always recorded, so a multi-hour print stays small. In "triggered raw" mode, full-rate samples are only kept around events (and a few seconds before and after). "Deviation" and "noise" are measured against a slowly-adapting baseline, so thermal drift does not trigger events. Changes apply immediately, including to a running recording.

Hypothetical tilt and load model

      
An educational side-view thought experiment, not a reconstruction of this print. All values below are invented examples until measured. The angle is manually assumed and does not follow G-code or Moonraker. The tool axis and sensor are assumed rigidly aligned; the blue sensor, tip and bed are schematic, not measured geometry.

      

        
        
        
        
      

      

        
        
        
      

      
      

      

      
Selected path: no G-code loaded. The scenario inputs are independent of the path.

      
Hypothetical counts versus angle for the same entered force and gains (not a time plot or measured curve). White dot marks the assumed angle when within the plotted range.

      
      
Forces are hypothetical forces on the tip in the fixed Y-Z plane. The model projects them onto the assumed tool axis and lateral axis, adds the moment about the sensor due to the chosen lever, and applies the entered sensitivities. The yellow arrow shows direction only. The model output is tare-free example counts; it is not the measured trace, a calibrated force, or contact pressure. It does not model friction, thermal drift, inertia, extrusion, contact area, other axes or nozzle collision. The alternative force may not be physically possible at a particular contact.

      

        How to characterize a real assembly
        
First record unloaded baselines at each independently verified angle and operating temperature, including cable and fan conditions. With a safe independent force reference and a known contact point, measure repeated loading and unloading at several force levels and opposing directions; repeat at positive and negative angles. Compare drift, repeatability, hysteresis and held-out measurements before considering a calibration. Repeat at different lever arms or use independent moment measurements to distinguish lateral sensitivity from bending-moment sensitivity. A single fixed-lever experiment can identify only their combined effect.

        
Compare in-air motion and extrusion with contact tests to identify confounding effects, and mark heating failures, pauses or invalid print regions separately. An assumed or commanded angle, a single scalar trace, and an endpoint fit do not establish the executed tilt, contact force or contact area. Do not use this illustration to control the printer.

The estimate assumes a standard Wheatstone-bridge load cell read by the ADS131M02 (a 1 count LSB is 143 nV / gain). Mellow does not publish the ALPS sensor or gain, so treat it as an order of magnitude only; a known-mass calibration always overrides it. Default is raw counts.
